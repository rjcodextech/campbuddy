# 3.4 Interest-based matching (Explore → People)

[← Index](00-index.md) · Previous: [3.3 Attendee roster ingestion](03-roster-ingestion.md) · Next: [3.5 Schedule & notifications →](05-notifications.md)

The architecture note in [8.3](../08-security-privacy.md#83-matching-data--the-local-by-default-shared-only-by-choice-architecture) governs this section: onboarding answers ([3.11](11-onboarding.md)) are never automatically public. A user must take a distinct, explicit action to become discoverable, and that action must be reversible.

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| M1 | The attendee's **primary profile** — everything from onboarding ([3.11](11-onboarding.md)): interests, why they're attending, who they want to meet — lives locally on the device by default, same as any other onboarding answer. Filling it in does **not**, by itself, make the attendee discoverable to anyone. |
| M2 | A separate, explicit **"Join attendee discovery"** action is what actually publishes anything. Tapping it: (a) generates a random, non-guessable **public** `discovery_id` for this device (not tied to name/email/any real identifier) *and*, separately, a random **owner token** ([8.6](../08-security-privacy.md#86-discovery-api-ownership--a-real-gap-caught-and-fixed-here)) that only this device ever holds, (b) walks the attendee through choosing exactly which fields to expose (profession, interest tags, "who I want to meet" — not the full onboarding answer set by default), (c) publishes only that chosen subset under the public `discovery_id`, scoped to the current event. |
| M3 | Matching runs **only** between attendees who've both taken the M2 action — never against the scraped roster's bare name/gravatar entries ([3.3](03-roster-ingestion.md)), which have no interest data and never opted into CampBuddy discovery at all ([finding 0.4](../00-findings.md)). A fixed, curated tag set (e.g. "content creator," "developer," "blogger") drives the match, not free text. |
| M4 | A matched profile shows only the fields that attendee chose to expose in M2, plus a photo if they added one — never their full local onboarding answers. If that same person separately appears in the ingested roster ([3.3](03-roster-ingestion.md)) under a matching name, their WordCamp-listed social links may be shown as a clearly separated "also found publicly" section, distinct from what they chose to expose via CampBuddy. |
| M5 | "I met them" marks a match as met; met people drop out of the primary match list but remain visible in a "people you've met" history (local to the device that marked it). |
| M6 | A visible, one-tap **"Leave attendee discovery"** action immediately un-publishes the discovery profile — distinct from, and simpler than, deleting all local data ([3.13](13-data-controls.md)). Leaving discovery keeps the attendee's local onboarding answers and Quest/My Day progress untouched; it only removes them from other attendees' match results. Leaving (and any later update to the published profile) requires the owner token from M2, not just the public `discovery_id` — see [8.6](../08-security-privacy.md#86-discovery-api-ownership--a-real-gap-caught-and-fixed-here) for why this distinction is load-bearing, not pedantic. |
| M7 | Discovery profiles carry an **optional expiry** tied to the event's end — once an event moves to `archived` ([7](../07-data-model.md)), its discovery profiles are purged automatically, same as the roster-purge policy in [8.4](../08-security-privacy.md#84-ingested-roster-data--explicit-policy). Nobody's matching data outlives the WordCamp it was created for unless they re-opt-in at a future event. *(Current implementation: profiles are stamped with the event's real last day + 3 retention days, and are never hidden earlier while the event is retained — [23](../23-data-retention.md).)* |
| M8 | The owner token is never displayed, never logged, never sent to analytics, and never recoverable if the device's local storage is cleared without first leaving discovery ([3.13](13-data-controls.md) DP3 already prompts for this) — losing it means the profile has to sit until its M7 expiry rather than being explicitly withdrawable. This tradeoff is accepted because the alternative (an account system to recover it) violates the no-accounts product decision ([2.3](../02-scope.md#23-explicitly-out-of-scope)). |

> **Current implementation note:** a compact version of this join/status flow (not the full match list) is also surfaced directly on [Home](01-home.md) under "Meet people," reusing the same code as the full Explore → People experience so there's one source of truth either way.
>
> **"Who's attending" (the roster, M3/M4):** `Api\RosterController` paginates at 200/request (bounding any single response, not limiting what the attendee sees) — `people.js`'s `fetchFullRoster()` walks every page in parallel and renders the complete roster as one flat list in `#people-roster`'s normal flow, no inner scrollbox. Fixed from an earlier version that only fetched page 1 (50/request), silently hiding every attendee past the 50th alphabetically for any event with a larger roster. **Row layout (phones):** each row is three columns — photo | name with the 👋 Open to meet pill and social icons stacked under it | a fixed-width **+ Meet** / **✓ To meet** button (`.roster-row`, `tpl-roster-row`). The icons sit under the name rather than beside it so the name keeps the width and wraps between words (only one endless word may break mid-word); the roster card also gives its side padding back to the rows so the middle column is as wide as it can be.

> **Current implementation note (who you are in discovery — supersedes "never your name" in M2):** anonymous-only profiles made matches unusable in practice — a card with three tags and no name can't be found in a room of 500 people. Joining now asks **"How should people see you?"** with three choices, the attendee's own decision each time:
>
> 1. **Pick my name from the attendee list** — search this event's public attendee list (the roster, [3.3](03-roster-ingestion.md)) and tap your name. The profile then shows *that entry's* public name, Gravatar and links (the same data already on the WordCamp's own Attendees page), marked "✓ On this WordCamp's attendee list". The name can't be typed over: the list's name always wins.
> 2. **Type my name** — for someone not on the list (60 characters max, control characters stripped). Shown as typed, without the attendee-list mark.
> 3. **Stay anonymous** — interests only, exactly as before.
>
> Plus an optional **WordPress.org username** (a pasted `profiles.wordpress.org/…` link is reduced to the username), shown as a "WordPress.org" button linking to their profile.
>
> **One name, one profile:** `discovery_profiles.attendee_roster_id` is unique, so a name already claimed can't be picked again (the picker shows it as "Already linked"; the API answers 422). Leaving discovery frees the name. Suppressing an attendee-list entry — self-service removal ([8.4](../08-security-privacy.md)) or an admin's Suppress — also unlinks it. Admin → Roster marks claimed names **In discovery** with an **Unlink** action, for when the real person says "that wasn't me".
>
> **Match list:** *Your best matches* (sharing at least one tag, strongest first, named people before anonymous ones, shared tags called out as "You both: …") → *Also open to meet* (everyone else who joined) → *People you've met*. Explore's "Who's attending" list marks names that joined discovery with **👋 Open to meet**. What's public is defined in one place: `DiscoveryProfile::publicCard()`.

> **Current implementation note — long match lists start short (Sept 2026):** on Explore → People, "Your best matches" and "Also open to meet" show only their first **3** cards; the rest are on the page but `hidden`, behind a **"Show N more"** button, where N is exactly how many cards that tap opens (ten, or fewer on the last tap — e.g. five cards read "Show 2 more" and open two), until none are left. Each card is opened once: no card is added, repeated or closed again by a tap, so nothing is ever duplicated (`resources/js/attendee/list-window.js`, used by `renderMatches`). Why: every match is a tall card, and with dozens of joiners the attendee list below ("Who's attending") was pushed thousands of pixels down (measured with 41 profiles at 390 px: it started at 10,435 px; now 2,031 px). Rules: a list of 4 or fewer is left exactly as it was (no button for one hidden card); people whose wave is waiting for an answer always show, whatever the limit; "You both want to meet" and "People you've met" are never cut. What is deliberately **not** changed: the cards, Wave, + Meet, I met them, the API, anything stored — the cards are all built and wired as before and only get `hidden`, so nothing they do changes. What an opened list looks like survives the re-render after a wave (kept in memory for the page visit; a new visit starts short). **Not built yet (post-event ideas):** a "Not for me" / discard action kept on the device, folding + Meet and I met them into a "⋯" menu so Wave is the one main button, a full-screen "all matches" panel with search, and unifying the `r:<id>` (attendee list) and `d:<id>` (discovery) keys so one person can't be added to My schedule twice.

> **Current implementation note — one status for a person, the same on every screen (Sept 2026):** Explore → People and My Day → My schedule now read and write **one record per person** (the `meetings` store on the device, `resources/js/attendee/people-state.js` describes it; `people-status.js` is the only code that changes it). Its `status` is `null` (planned — "+ Meet"), `met`, `missed` ("Couldn't meet") or `skipped` (reserved for hiding, see below). So: **I met them** on a match card = **✓ Met** in My Day (and the reverse); **✗ Couldn't** in My Day puts the match in a folded **"Couldn't meet (N) ▸"** section under the lists on Explore with a **Try again** button; a **✓ Met** card on Explore has an **Undo**; the Meet button reads "✓ To meet" / "✓ Met" / "Couldn't meet" by the same record — on the attendee list too (the list is where you *start*: "+ Meet" there makes the record, after which discovery and My Day follow it). Someone marked "I met them" who was never planned gets a record marked `unplanned`: they appear in My Day's *People to meet* as **Met** ("Met at the event", no invented time) but are **not part of "N of M done"** and not in the calendar export, until the Meet sheet's Save plans them. `metHistory` (the older "I met them" list) is still written, and still counts for someone with no record; once a record exists it rules, so an Undo is not undone by the old list. Nothing is ever deleted by these actions. A page that was in the background redraws when it comes back, only if a record changed. **Known gap:** the attendee list keys a person `r:<rosterId>` and a discovery match `d:<discoveryId>`; the same human can therefore be two records (needs the roster id in the discovery card, a small server change, not done).

> **Current implementation note — ✕ Hide (Sept 2026):** every match that isn't met has a small **✕** in its corner. It sets the person's status to `skipped` (see the note above): they leave *Your best matches / Also open to meet / You both want to meet*, and appear in a folded **"Hidden (N) ▸"** section under the lists (and under *Hidden* on My schedule) with a **Show again** button that gives back what they were (planned with their note and time, met, or couldn't-meet — `statusBeforeSkip`). **Nothing is deleted**; only "Clear my data" removes anything. Someone you are chatting with (mutual wave) is asked about first (`confirm`: "Hide? You'll stop seeing your chat with them."); anyone else hides at once with a toast. Hidden people don't count in Home's "N matches want to meet you". The other person is never told, and it only hides them from *your* view — they can still wave or write, you just won't see it (a real block would need the server). The Meet sheet's old **Remove** (which deleted the person's note and time) is now **Hide from plan**, the same status. Built in `people.js` (`hideCard`, `foldSection`, `personRow`) and `people-status.js`; the ✕ and the folded sections are made by JS, not templates.
